Colour

The ink scale is semantic, not a lightness ramp. Pick by role, never by how grey something feels — that habit is what let a section label end up lighter than the content it introduced.

Ink — three tiers

TierRoleSpecimen
--ink Titles and structural labels — the small tracked caps that name a section MAP VIEW
--ink-soft Content: body copy, legend rows, stat labels, dates Sprayed Volume
--ink-faint Notes about content: coordinates, axis years, table values 16.86°N 106.74°E

Structural labels take the darkest ink because size and tracking already separate them from their content — so colour is free to mark them as structure.

Ratios are measured against --paper at load, not quoted from memory. Data hues are data: they may not be borrowed for UI chrome. Agent White is a slate blue-grey on purpose — a neutral silver was indistinguishable from the context grey that carries the unselected record.

One display face, site-wide

There used to be a split — Gambarino for the Story, Playfair Display for the Archive — and it was a real source of confusion: two faces close enough at a glance to be mistaken for one. Gambarino is retired. Every serif on this page, the Story and the Archive is now Playfair Display, one token, no per-surface exception.

Playfair Display--font-serif

Ag The Archive · 1971

Handgloves quickly, 19.5M gallons

The one display voice. Masthead, section titles, card titles and years, quotes, deks, stat figures, panel title and subtitle, the playhead date, every anchor figure. High stroke contrast on a strict vertical axis — why it holds up as a figure and a headline, but not as running text below ~15px.

Gambarino stays declared in src/fonts.css only for a one-line rollback; no component references it, so it is never fetched. If you go looking for it in devtools anyway: the shipped Gambarino-Regular.woff2 reports its family name as the literal string false (the .woff build reports Gambarino) — a font inspector will call the face unnamed rather than missing, in the one place it still ships.

The serif rule

Serif is a display role, not a content type. It belongs to anchor text — 17px and up — and each surface gets exactly one anchor. Below 17px, everything is sans, no matter what the words refer to.

Rule One serif anchor; the notes below it are sans. The same date and the same kind of figure can be serif in one place and sans in another — that is the rule working, not an inconsistency.

The physical reason: Playfair Display carries high stroke contrast on a Didone-style vertical axis, and its thin strokes thin out towards a hairline below roughly 15px. Public Sans ships 300/400/500 only, so anything bolder is a synthesised faux-bold.

Caps and units

Initial Caps for every label, badge, chip and button. Uppercase survives in exactly two places, both signposts rather than content: 10px structural labels (MAP VIEW, JUMP TO) and map linework tags (MILITARY REGION III).

Write units as a word beside the figure, sharing a baseline — 81K Gallons. Prefer a round number to an ≈ glyph: 13 km Grid Cell says "about" by being round, and reads as a noun phrase parallel to Single Spray Run.

Surfaces

Two recipes, chosen by what is behind the panel. Glass is for panels over a map; paper is for panels over a page — backdrop-filter on a fixed panel re-samples the scrolling content beneath it every frame and flickers.

Two encodings, on purpose

The story and the Explorer render the same 24,604 spray runs two different ways, and the contrast between them is the product narrative.

SurfaceEncodingJob
StoryKDE heat fieldRain on land — feeling
ExplorerProportional symbols, area ∝ gallonsA countable record — inspection

Because the data is homogeneous — timestamped points, one category, one absolute quantity — the Explorer keeps one representational language at every zoom and changes only the aggregation cell: 0.12° (≈13 km) below z7, 0.03° (≈3 km) to z9.2, then the raw runs. Radius is k·√gallons so area, not radius, carries the quantity; there is no stroke, because overlap darkening by alpha is the closest WebGL gets to a multiply blend.

Selection greys the rest of the record rather than hiding it — context stays on the page, and the chart, the legend and the map all follow the same tint.
